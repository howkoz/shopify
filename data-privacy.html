Data Privacy & Compliance

GDPR, CCPA, and Japan APPI compliance configuration for customer data protection across all markets.

⚠️ Legal Disclaimer

This guide provides configuration guidance and best practices for data privacy compliance. Consult your legal team for all compliance decisions and regulatory requirements specific to your jurisdiction.

🌍 Privacy Landscape

Three major privacy frameworks govern customer data protection globally. Understanding each is essential for operating across international markets.

GDPR (European Union)

Scope: All EU residents

Jurisdiction: Most comprehensive privacy law

Enforcement: Fines up to 4% of global revenue

Key: Consent-based processing with extensive data subject rights

CCPA/CPRA (California)

Scope: Expanding US state-level privacy laws

Jurisdiction: California + 8+ similar state laws

Enforcement: Fines up to $7,500 per violation

Key: Consumer rights to know, delete, and opt-out

APPI (Japan)

Scope: Japanese personal information

Jurisdiction: Japan and cross-border transfers

Enforcement: Administrative penalties and restrictions

Key: Explicit consent for collection and cross-border transfers (updated 2022)

📋 Compliance Priorities

  • Consent Management: Explicit, documented consent for all processing activities
  • Data Minimization: Collect only necessary data for specified purposes
  • Transparency: Clear privacy policies and customer communication
  • Rights Fulfillment: Processes to handle data access, deletion, and portability requests
  • Security: Protection of personal data from unauthorized access or breach
  • Cross-Border Controls: Mechanisms for safe international data transfers

🇪🇺 GDPR Requirements

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is the most comprehensive privacy framework, applicable to any organization processing data of EU residents.

Lawful Basis for Processing

You must have one of these lawful bases to process personal data:

  • Consent: Explicit opt-in from the data subject
  • Contract: Processing necessary to fulfill a contract
  • Legal Obligation: Required by law (e.g., tax, employment laws)
  • Vital Interest: Protecting someone's life or health
  • Public Task: Exercising official authority
  • Legitimate Interest: Your organization's interests (least used, high risk)

Data Subject Rights

Right to Access (Article 15)

Customers can request all personal data held about them. Must respond within 30 days with data in machine-readable format.

Right to Erasure (Article 17)

"Right to be forgotten" - customers can request deletion. Exceptions: legal obligations, public interest, defense of legal claims.

Right to Portability (Article 20)

Customers can receive data in structured, commonly used, machine-readable format and transmit to another controller.

Right to Object (Article 21)

Customers can object to processing for marketing or legitimate interest purposes.

Right to Rectification (Article 16)

Customers can request correction of inaccurate personal data.

Rights Related to Automated Decision Making (Article 22)

Customers have rights regarding automated processing that produces legal or significant effects.

Consent Management

  • Explicit Opt-In: Required for consent-based processing
  • Double Opt-In for Email: Mandatory in Germany, strongly recommended everywhere in EU
  • Granular Consent: Separate consent for different uses (e.g., marketing vs. transactional)
  • Pre-Unchecked Boxes: Checkboxes must be unchecked by default (cannot pre-check consent)
  • Withdrawal: Easy mechanism to withdraw consent at any time

Key Obligations

Data Protection Officer (DPO)

Required if: public authority, large-scale systematic monitoring, or regular large-scale processing of special categories of data.

Privacy Impact Assessments

Required for high-risk processing (automated decision making, large-scale processing, special categories of data).

Breach Notification

Notify supervisory authority within 72 hours of discovering a breach affecting personal data. Notify affected individuals without undue delay if high risk.

Data Processing Agreements (DPA)

Required with all vendors/processors who handle customer data. Must specify data types, purposes, and security measures.

Cross-Border Transfer Rules

Transferring data outside the EU requires legal mechanisms:

  • Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs): EU-approved contract terms for non-adequate countries
  • Adequacy Decisions: EU finding that destination country has adequate privacy protection
  • Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs): Internal policies for multi-national organizations
  • Current Status: US transfers require SCCs (not automated mechanisms alone)

🇺🇸 CCPA/CPRA Requirements

California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) and California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA) grant California residents extensive privacy rights. Related laws now exist in 8+ additional states.

Applicability

CCPA applies to for-profit businesses that collect California residents' personal information AND meet one of these thresholds:

  • Annual revenue exceeding $25 million
  • Buy, sell, or share personal information of 100,000+ residents or households
  • Derive 50%+ of annual revenue from selling/sharing residents' personal information

Consumer Rights

Right to Know (CCPA § 1798.100)

Consumers can request what personal information is collected, used, shared, or sold. Must respond within 45 days with specific details.

Right to Delete (CCPA § 1798.105)

Consumers can request deletion of personal information. Exceptions: fraud prevention, security, legal obligations, internal analytics.

Right to Opt-Out (CCPA § 1798.120)

Consumers can opt-out of sale or sharing of personal information. Implement "Do Not Sell My Personal Information" link on homepage.

Right to Correct (CPRA - new)

Consumers can request correction of inaccurate personal information.

Right to Limit Use (CPRA - new)

Consumers can limit use of personal information to necessary business purposes.

Right to Non-Discrimination (CCPA § 1798.125)

Cannot discriminate against consumers for exercising CCPA rights. Cannot deny service, charge higher prices, or provide lower quality for exercising rights.

Data Request Handling

  • Timeline: Must respond within 45 calendar days
  • Lookback Period: 12 months of data
  • Verification: Must verify consumer identity (balance security with accessibility)
  • Format: Provide data in structured, commonly used format (CSV, JSON preferred)
  • Frequency: Consumers can submit requests twice per 12-month period

Required Disclosures

Privacy policy must include (in California):

  • Categories of personal information collected
  • Purposes for collection and use
  • Categories of sources of personal information
  • Categories of third parties with whom information is shared
  • How to submit requests (access, deletion, opt-out)
  • Categories of information sold/shared and opt-out instructions
  • Financial incentives and how to opt-out

Required Links and Notices

"Do Not Sell My Personal Information"

Required on homepage and privacy policy. Link must be conspicuous (same font size and color as other links).

"Limit Use of My Sensitive Personal Information" (CPRA)

Required for consumers in California regarding sensitive data use.

Shine the Light Notice (optional but recommended)

California Civil Code § 1798.83 requires disclosure of third parties sharing info for direct marketing.

🇯🇵 Japan APPI Requirements

The Act on the Protection of Personal Information (APPI) governs personal information collection and use in Japan. Amended in 2022 with stronger requirements.

Scope and Applicability

  • Applies to organizations holding personal information of Japanese residents
  • Personal information: Information by which individuals can be identified
  • Includes names, addresses, phone numbers, email, ID numbers, IP addresses
  • Exception: Anonymized data (cannot be restored to identify individuals)

Collection and Consent Requirements

Consent Required for Collection

Generally, explicit consent required before collecting personal information, with limited exceptions for contractual necessity and legal obligations.

Purpose Notification

Must clearly state the purpose for collecting personal information. Cannot use data for purpose different than stated.

Accurate Data

Must maintain accuracy of personal information and take reasonable steps to ensure it's up-to-date.

Cross-Border Transfer Rules (Updated 2022)

Most significant APPI requirement: Strict controls on transferring personal information outside Japan

  • Explicit Consent Required: Must obtain explicit consent from individual for cross-border transfers
  • No Default Assumption: Cannot assume consent for international transfers (e.g., cloud services)
  • Adequacy Findings: Transfer to countries with PPC adequacy decisions (EU, South Korea, Philippines as of 2024) has lighter requirements
  • Non-Adequate Countries: Requires documented contractual commitments and individual consent for transfers to US, China, Singapore, etc.

Data Subject Rights

Right to Access

Individuals can request access to personal information held about them.

Right to Correction

Individuals can request correction of inaccurate information.

Right to Deletion

Individuals can request deletion of information (subject to exceptions).

Right to Suspend Use

Individuals can request suspension of use for purposes other than those stated at collection.

Special Categories (Enhanced Protection)

The following require explicit consent:

  • Race, ethnicity, or national origin
  • Religious or political beliefs
  • Medical information or health status
  • Criminal record information
  • Information indicating sexual orientation or identity

Breach Notification

  • Notification Required: If breach affects security of personal information
  • Timeline: Without undue delay (typically within 30 days)
  • Content: Description of breach, affected data, likely impact, remedial measures
  • Authority Report: Notify Personal Information Protection Commission if significant scope

Key Differences from GDPR/CCPA

  • Cross-Border Transfers: More restrictive than GDPR/CCPA; cannot rely on standard clauses alone
  • Purpose Limitation: Strict - cannot easily repurpose collected data
  • Anonymization: Properly anonymized data has fewer restrictions
  • Joint Use Disclosure: Must disclose when sharing data with other organizations in advance

⚙️ Shopify Configuration

Step-by-step guidance for configuring Shopify Plus for privacy compliance across all markets.

1. Cookie Consent Management

Shopify Built-In Cookie Banner

Path: Settings > Customer privacy > Cookie consent

Configuration:

  • Enable cookie banner (required for EU visitors)
  • Set three consent levels: Essential only, Analytics, Marketing
  • Essential cookies: Required for checkout and security (cannot be disabled)
  • Analytics: Google Analytics, session tracking
  • Marketing: Advertising pixels, remarketing, email collection
Third-Party Cookie Tools

For advanced requirements, integrate: OneTrust, Cookiebot, or TrustArc

  • Provides granular cookie categorization
  • Supports geolocation-based consent (different rules per country)
  • Consent management platform (CMP) compliance

2. Customer Data Requests

Shopify Webhooks

Shopify automatically sends webhooks for customer data requests (GDPR/CCPA compliance):

• customers/data_request
• customers/redact
• shop/redact

Response Requirement: 30 days (GDPR), 45 days (CCPA)

Privacy Policy Template

Path: Settings > Legal > Privacy policy

  • Auto-generated template covering basic requirements
  • Must be customized for your specific data practices
  • Should mention third-party apps with data access
  • Must include cross-border transfer details (especially for Japan)

3. Marketing Consent Configuration

Email Consent

EU Markets (Recommended):

  • Double opt-in: Confirmation email after signup
  • Mandatory in Germany, strongly recommended in all EU
  • Checkbox at checkout: "Email me with news and offers" (UNCHECKED by default)
  • Pre-checked boxes = non-compliance in most EU countries

Implementation: Klaviyo, Klaviyo Flow, or custom checkbox logic

SMS Consent (Separate)

SMS consent must be separate from email consent

  • Explicit opt-in required for SMS marketing
  • Record consent timestamp and method
  • Easy opt-out mechanism required

4. Data Retention Policy

Recommended Retention Periods
Customer account data: Active + 3 years after last purchase
Order data: 7 years (financial/accounting requirements)
Transactional emails: 7 years
Analytics data: 26 months (GA4 default)
Marketing engagement data: 12-24 months
Support/help desk tickets: 3-5 years
Payment card data: Never store (PCI DSS violation)

5. App Permissions Audit

Review All Installed Apps

Path: Settings > Apps and integrations

  • List all apps with customer data access
  • Verify each app's privacy policy and DPA
  • Document data types: Orders, customers, email, payment info
  • Confirm security certifications (SOC 2, ISO 27001)
Data Processing Agreements (DPA)

Required with every app that processes customer data:

  • Specify data types and volume
  • Define processing purposes
  • Outline security and confidentiality measures
  • Address sub-processors and international transfers
  • Include customer rights obligations (access, deletion, etc.)

6. Cross-Border Transfer Mechanisms

For EU to US Transfers (Most Common)
  • Implement Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) with all US-based vendors
  • Shopify has SCCs in place; verify with any third-party apps
  • Document transfer mechanism in your privacy policy
For Japan to Anywhere Transfers (Critical)
  • Require explicit customer consent before transferring data outside Japan
  • Exceptions: Adequacy-finding countries (EU, South Korea, Philippines)
  • For other destinations: Documented contractual commitments required
  • Must be disclosed in Japanese privacy policy

7. Security Configuration

Shopify Plus Security Features
  • Enable 2FA for all admin accounts
  • Use custom SSL certificate and HTTPS enforced
  • Restrict staff access (principle of least privilege)
  • Enable audit logs for all data access
  • Configure access control policies for sensitive data

🔄 Customer Data Rights Workflow

Standard process for handling customer requests for data access, deletion, or portability across all markets.

Data Request Handling Process

Request Received

Customer submits request via Shopify form, email, or Shopify API webhook. Record: request type (access/delete/object), timestamp, channel, customer identity.

Verify Identity

Confirm customer identity by email address, order number, or other verifiable information. Document verification method. (Balance security with accessibility - avoid asking for sensitive information like passwords.)

Acknowledge & Timeline

Send immediate acknowledgment email. State processing timeline: 30 days (GDPR), 45 days (CCPA), 30 days (APPI). Provide reference number for tracking.

Gather Data

Collect personal data from all systems: Shopify (orders, customer profile, interactions), ERP system, CRM, marketing platform (Klaviyo, Omnisend), email archive, support tickets (Zendesk, Help Scout), payment processor logs.

Compile Response

For access requests: Export in structured format (CSV, JSON). For deletion: Execute deletion across all systems. For portability: Machine-readable format (JSON preferred). Remove sensitive fields like internal notes or third-party IDs where appropriate.

Respond & Document

Deliver response within deadline. Send via secure channel (encrypted email, secure link). Document: response date, method, data included, any limitations. Store documentation for 3 years (audit trail).

Update Records

If deletion approved, confirm deletion across all systems. Mark customer as "deletion requested" in audit logs. Note: legal holds may require retaining some data despite deletion requests.

🚨 Breach Response Timeline

Incident response plan for personal data breaches. Timing varies by jurisdiction.

Hour 0-1: Identify & Contain

Detect breach, isolate affected systems, secure evidence. Determine: What data was exposed? How many individuals? Which markets affected?

Hour 1-4: Assess Scope

Confirm data types (names, emails, payment data, SSNs, etc.), number of affected customers per country, whether special categories involved (health, race, financial).

Hour 4-24: Internal Notification

Alert leadership, legal team, security team. Engage legal counsel. Begin root cause analysis. Notify insurance provider if applicable.

Hour 24-72: Regulatory Notification (GDPR Critical)

GDPR Requirement: Notify supervisory authority within 72 hours if high risk. EU country with most affected customers has jurisdiction. Report includes: incident details, impact assessment, remedial measures.

Day 3-30: Customer Notification

If high risk to individuals: Notify affected customers without undue delay. Content: Nature of breach, affected data, remedial measures, contact info for questions. Notification method: email or website notice (mail if emails compromised).

Day 30+: Post-Incident

Complete root cause analysis, implement remediation, notify affected customers if not yet done (CCPA 15 days), document all actions, review insurance coverage, board report.

Key Breach Response Contacts

  • EU Supervisory Authority: Data Protection Authority of affected country(ies)
  • US Breach Laws: State Attorney General (varies by state); AG contact info on https://www.naag.org
  • Japan PPC: Personal Information Protection Commission (https://www.ppc.go.jp)
  • Legal Counsel: Data privacy/cybersecurity specialist
  • Insurance: Cyber liability carrier
