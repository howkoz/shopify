Bottom Line

Shopify Plus is a strong operational fit for the Mississippi pilot. POS, tax, and channel management work out of the box. The team solved the POS subscription enrollment gap — previously the biggest product limitation. The remaining gaps (two-tier pricing, Unity sync, processing rates at scale) all have working solutions. The honest concern is Phase 2: workarounds need to be replaced with robust solutions before scaling to 200 distributors.

✅ Pros
📱
Tap to Pay on iPhone — No Hardware
Distributors use their own iPhones. Zero card reader hardware to purchase, ship, or replace. Works on any NFC-capable iPhone running iOS 16+.
🌐
Works Offline
POS continues in low-signal areas and syncs when connectivity restores. Critical for field sales in rural Mississippi markets.
💳
Full Payment Method Support
Tap to Pay, chip, swipe, manual entry, and cash all work natively. Distributors handle any customer scenario.
🧾
Automatic Tax on Every Transaction
Shopify Tax calculates the correct rate based on customer location automatically. Zero manual tax lookup required.
👥
Role-Based Access
POS staff roles limit distributors to sales only. Regional managers get elevated access. Prevents accidental admin changes.
📊
Unified Dashboard Across All Channels
Online, POS, and headless storefront orders all appear in one Shopify Admin view. No cross-system reconciliation needed.
⚠️ Cons
🔒
Card Issuer Fraud Blocks on New Merchant Name
Contactless declines happen when issuers flag an unrecognized merchant. Customers must call their bank or use chip/swipe. Fades as merchant reputation builds (30–60 days).
💰
Processing Rates Higher Than Unicity's Current
Shopify Plus runs 2.15% + $0.30 vs Unicity's negotiated 1.30%. At $84M annual card volume that's ~$714K/year more. Fine for pilot; must resolve before full migration.
🆔
Distributor Attribution Relies on Staff Login
POS attributes orders to the logged-in staff member. Every distributor must have their own login and must not share devices. Fragile at scale without enforcement.
💸
Shopify Plus Fee: $2,300/Month
At 10 pilot distributors this is high overhead per transaction. Cost per transaction drops significantly at 200 distributors doing real volume.
🔧
OAuth Token Friction for Send to POS
The "Send to POS" flow required custom OAuth token resolution — a technical blocker that added implementation time and maintenance overhead.

Key Architectural Insight: Unidirectional Data Flow
Shopify does not need to store Unicity distributor IDs. Data flows one way — Shopify to Unity. Email is the join key between systems. This eliminated complex round-trip data mapping and is the most important architectural decision in this project.
✅ Pros
📧
Email as Universal Join Key
Every Shopify order contains customer email. Unity uses the same email. No custom ID mapping — the sync query joins on email naturally.
🔗
Webhook-Based Near-Real-Time Sync
Shopify fires order webhooks on creation and payment, triggering the sync to Unity immediately without scheduled batch polling.
🛠️
Well-Documented Admin API
Comprehensive REST and GraphQL APIs. Every order, customer, and product field is accessible. API versioning means breaking changes are predictable and announced.
🔄
Apps Script Covers Phase 1
Daily sync automation works without dedicated infrastructure. Sufficient for pilot scale (10–50 distributors).
⚠️ Cons
🏗️
No Native Shopify-to-Unity Connector
Everything is custom built. Every data field mapping requires manual specification, development, and testing against both systems.
⚠️
Apps Script Has Scale Limits
Google Apps Script hits execution time limits at high order volume. Phase 2 plan requires migration to Node.js — adding future engineering work.
🔕
Sync Failures Are Silent Without Monitoring
If the webhook or Apps Script fails, orders may not reach Unity. No native alerting — requires custom logging and monitoring to catch failures before they impact commissions or fulfillment.
🗃️
Unity Is Still System of Record
Shopify is downstream. Any Unity schema change requires updating the sync logic. Two systems to maintain in perpetuity.

Core Problem: Shopify Thinks in SKUs, Unicity Thinks in Tiers
Shopify's native pricing is one price per product variant. Unicity needs two prices — wholesale for members, retail for the public. Because the storefront is headless, native customer group pricing doesn't work. The solution is separate product listings per tier with JavaScript visibility logic. It works but doubles catalog complexity.
✅ Pros
🔧
Workaround Is Functional
Separate member/retail product listings with JS visibility logic is fully implemented and validated through live test transactions.
💵
Price Lock at Variant Level
Once the correct product variant is selected, Shopify enforces that price at checkout with no risk of the wrong price slipping through.
📦
4 Pilot Products Loaded and Priced
All four initial products are in Shopify with both pricing tiers configured and validated.
⚠️ Cons
📂
Doubled Catalog Complexity
Every product requires two listings. At 4 products this is manageable. At 40 products it becomes a maintenance burden — updates must hit both versions.
⚔️
Bundles App Conflicts with Subscriptions
Shopify's native Bundles app is incompatible with subscription functionality. Feel Great 2.0 bundle structure can't use both simultaneously.
🌍
International Pricing Not Addressed
Current setup is US-only. Future international markets need currency, tax, and pricing rules per country — Shopify Markets helps but adds complexity.

Rate Reality: Shopify Payments Costs Unicity More at Scale
Unicity's current negotiated card rate is 1.30%. Shopify Plus runs 2.15% + $0.30. Stripe standard is 2.90% + $0.30. At $84M in annual card volume, switching fully to Shopify Payments adds ~$714K/year. Moving to Stripe standard rates adds ~$1.7M/year. The pilot is low volume so this isn't a blocker now — but it must be resolved before any full platform migration.

⚠️ Shop Pay Is Not a Standalone Service
Shop Pay cannot be added to non-Shopify sites. It requires Shopify Payments enabled, a Shopify storefront (or headless via Storefront API), and a merchant in a Shopify Payments supported country. It is not like Apple Pay or Google Pay that work anywhere. You only get Shop Pay by committing to Shopify as the commerce platform.

Active Risks
High
Shopify–Unity Sync Failure — Orders Lost in Transit
If Apps Script fails silently, orders processed in Shopify don't reach Unity. Distributors miss commissions and fulfillment breaks. No automated alerting currently exists.
→ Add failure logging to Apps Script, set up email alerts on sync errors, build daily reconciliation report comparing Shopify order count vs Unity order count.
Medium
Card Issuer Fraud Blocks During Initial Launch Window
New merchant name triggers declines during the first 30–60 days. Distributors need to handle objections confidently in the field.
→ Brief all distributors with the chip/swipe fallback script before first sales. Issue fades naturally as merchant history builds.
Medium
Catalog Maintenance Doubles as Product Line Grows
The two-listing approach (member + retail) means every catalog update requires two edits. At scale this creates version drift where one tier gets updated but the other doesn't.
→ Create a catalog update SOP. Investigate Shopify Price Lists (Plus feature) as a longer-term native solution.
Medium
Apps Script → Node.js Migration Gets Deprioritized
Phase 2 requires moving to Node.js for sync reliability at scale. If deprioritized, technical debt accumulates and failure risk increases with order volume.
→ Set a formal trigger: if daily orders exceed X or Apps Script fails more than Y times/month, escalate to Node.js migration automatically.
Medium
Processing Rate Gap Becomes Expensive at Full Scale
At $84M+ annual card volume, Shopify Payments at 2.15% vs Unicity's 1.30% represents ~$714K/year in additional costs. Negligible at pilot scale, material at full migration.
→ Request Shopify enterprise rate negotiation before Phase 2. Evaluate whether Shop Pay conversion lift offsets the rate difference.
Low
Replit Hosting Stability for Headless Storefront
Colby's store runs on Replit. Downtime or pricing changes take the storefront offline. POS operations are unaffected.
→ Move to Vercel or Netlify before Phase 2 launch.
Low
Shopify Price or Policy Change
Shopify Plus pricing and API terms can change. At $2,300/month already, further increases affect unit economics.
→ Annual contract negotiation, monitor Shopify changelog. Not an immediate concern.

Final Assessment: Proceed with Shopify Plus

Shopify Plus is the right platform for this pilot. It solves the hard problems — POS payments, tax compliance, channel management, and API extensibility — out of the box. The team resolved the POS subscription enrollment gap, which was the biggest product limitation. The remaining gaps (two-tier pricing, Unity sync, processing rates at scale) all have working solutions. The honest concern is Phase 2: workarounds need to be replaced before scaling to 200 distributors.
